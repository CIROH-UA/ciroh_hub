The U.S. National Water Model produces an overwhelming amount of data,
with its coverage of 2.7 million river reaches producing about five
terabytes of output daily. This data has the potential to be immensely
useful to the hydrologic community, but to do so, researchers and
operational stakeholders need to actually get access to that data.
This is far easier said than done in practice. In addition to an avenue
for retrieving and processing the data, would-be users need to be able
to trust and assess the data, reproduce the process of creating or
performing operations over the dataset, and have the knowledge to take
full advantage of the available tools that fulfill these needs.

[CIROH's Hydroinformatics Working Group](https://hub.ciroh.org/working-groups/hydroinformatics)
has aimed to address these challenges through the development of improved
architectures for data retrieval, evaluation tools, software organization,
and accessibility. This November, they published their efforts in the
Journal of the American Water Resources Association as
"[Bridging the Research-to-Operations Gaps in NOAA Water Prediction Through
Advances in Hydroinformatics](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1752-1688.70161)".
This publication validates the importance of this important work, as well
as making these advances fully visible to the broader hydrologic community.
